2003 — Born in Beijing

I don't remember it, obviously. Nobody remembers being born. But my mom tells it the same way every time, so it's kind of become my memory too. It was winter in Beijing, really dry, and she says the hospital hallway smelled like boiled water and those orange peels people put on the radiators. My dad was late because his bike chain came off on the way. He still gets annoyed when she brings that up. And my grandmother had already picked out a name, and then my parents just... didn't use it, and she didn't talk to them for a week. I think about that a lot, actually. That before I could do anything there were already, like, three different stories about who I was going to be. Um. When I go back now the hospital isn't even there anymore. It's a mall, I think. So the place only exists in how she tells it.

2009 — First camera

It was a little silver digital camera, the kind that slid open. It was my uncle's, and he gave it to me because he'd bought a new one, so it wasn't even a gift exactly, it was more like a hand-me-down. I was six. And I took pictures of the worst things. Like the floor. The corner of the kitchen. My own feet on the bus. The screen on the back was so small and kind of green, so everything looked like it was underwater. My mom would scroll through and go, why is there no one in these? And I didn't have an answer then. I think I just liked that I could keep a corner of a room. That nobody else thought it was worth keeping, and I did. I still have the memory card somewhere. I'm almost scared to look at it, honestly, in case it's empty.

2012 — The first urge to write a story

I was nine, maybe, and it was summer, and the power went out in our building. The whole afternoon. It was so hot that we were all just sitting on the stairwell because it was the coolest place. And the old man from upstairs started telling this story about a fox that lived in a well, and he never finished it, he just got called inside. And I was so... I was almost angry about it. So that night I wrote the ending myself, in this exercise book with the grid lines, the kind you use for practicing characters. It was terrible. The fox just gets out of the well and goes home. But I remember the feeling of the pencil going faster than I could think. Like the story already knew where it was going and I was just running after it. I've been trying to get that feeling back pretty much ever since.

2016 — Wanted to be a fashion designer

So I was thirteen and I was completely sure I was going to be a fashion designer. Like, sure in the way you're only sure about things at thirteen. I had this notebook full of girls with impossibly long legs, all wearing coats. Only coats. I don't know why, I was obsessed with collars. And I would take my mom's old scarves and pin them onto a pillow because I didn't have a mannequin, and then I'd make her come look at the pillow. She was very patient about the pillow. Um, I didn't become a fashion designer. But I think it was the first time I noticed that I cared about how something falls. Like how fabric hangs off a shoulder, how there's a weight to it. That stayed. That's still most of what I care about, honestly, it just moved into other things.

2017 — Read a book that changed my life

It's hard to say which book without it sounding like a cliché, so I won't. But I read it on the train going to my grandmother's, and the train was maybe four hours, and I finished it right before we got in. And I just sat there while everyone was getting their bags down, because I couldn't move yet. I felt like someone had described a room inside me that I didn't know had a door. There was one sentence, I wrote it on my hand, and then I washed my hands at her place without thinking and it was gone. And I never found it again. I mean, it's in the book, I could look. But I don't want to. I like that the version I remember might not be what it actually says. That I kind of rewrote it by losing it.

2020 — The year everything slowed down

Everyone has a version of that year, I know. For me it was that the days stopped having edges. You'd wake up and it would be the afternoon, somehow. I was in my last year of high school, all on a laptop, with the camera off most of the time. And the thing I remember isn't the fear, really, although there was that. It's the window. Our living room had this one window where the light came in at like four o'clock and crossed the floor really slowly, and I started watching it every day. Like it was a show. I'd lie on the floor and watch the rectangle move over the rug, onto the table leg, up the wall. I started taking a photo of it every day at the same time. I think that was the first time I understood that just paying attention could be the whole thing. That you didn't have to make anything.

2021 — Started designing seriously

I'd been making stuff for a long time, but there's a difference between playing and actually sitting down and saying, okay, this is the thing I do now. For me that was a poster. It was for a friend's band, a really small show, and I spent, I think, three weeks on it. Which is insane for a poster. I kept moving one line of text like two pixels to the left and then back. And at some point it was three in the morning and I realized I wasn't tired, and I wasn't doing it for anyone, I was doing it because it bothered me that it wasn't right yet. That kind of bother. Um. Maybe twelve people came to the show. Nobody looked at the poster. But I printed one for myself and it's still on my wall, a bit faded now. It's not good. But it's where it started.

2021 — Wrote something true for the first time

I'd written a lot before that, but it was always, like, performing. Trying to sound like writers I liked. And then one night I wrote about my dad. Just a small thing, about how he peels apples in one long strip and hands you the whole apple without saying anything, and that's how he says sorry. It was maybe half a page. And when I read it back I felt kind of sick, honestly, because there was nothing to hide behind. No pretty sentences. It was just what was true. I didn't show anyone for months. When I finally showed it to a teacher, she didn't say anything about the writing, she just said, does he know? And I said no. And he still doesn't. But I think about how that's the first thing I wrote that I couldn't have made up.

2022 — Left a place I thought I'd stay longer

There was this apartment. It wasn't anything special, the radiator knocked all night and the window over the sink didn't close all the way. But it was the first place that was mine, and I had this plan that I'd be there for years. I'd painted one wall this pale green. I'd figured out exactly which stair creaked. And then things changed, for reasons that aren't really interesting, and I had like three weeks to pack. The last night I sat on the floor because the furniture was already gone, and the room sounded completely different. Hollow. Like it had already forgotten me. Um. I left one thing behind on purpose, a little dried flower tucked above the door frame. I don't know if anyone ever found it. I like thinking it's still up there.

2023 — Started learning to trust my own eye

I used to ask everyone. Every time I made something I'd send it to like five people. Is this okay, which one is better, should the red be less red. And I'd get five different answers and end up making something that was kind of an average of everyone, which is to say it wasn't anybody's. Then there was this one project where I just ran out of time to ask. I had to decide alone. And I chose this photograph that was a little out of focus, a little too dark, that I knew people would tell me to replace. And it was the right one. I knew it the way you know a song is in tune. It wasn't that I suddenly got confident, I'm still not, really. It's more that I noticed there's a quiet voice that's been right more often than I gave it credit for. I'm learning to wait for it.

2024 — A friendship that quietly ended

There wasn't a fight. I think that's the hardest part to explain to people, because they always ask what happened, and nothing did. We used to talk every day, and then every week, and then it was only birthdays, and then I realized I'd missed hers and she hadn't said anything. And neither of us did anything about it. We'd been friends since we were eleven. We had this whole language, words that only meant something to us. And now there's no one alive who knows what those words mean except two people who don't talk. Um. Sometimes I start typing to her and I don't know what I'd even say. I'm not angry. I'm not even sure I'm sad, exactly. It's more like a room in a house you used to live in. You know it's still there. You just don't have the key anymore.

2024 — Showed work I wasn't sure about

It was a small group show, and I almost pulled my piece the night before. It was a series of photographs of hands, mostly blurry, and I kept thinking, this is too quiet, nobody's going to stop for this. My hands were shaking when I hung it, which is kind of funny given what it was. And for most of the opening, yeah, people walked past. I stood by the drinks pretending not to watch. And then this older woman stood in front of it for a really long time. Like ten minutes. And she didn't come find me or anything, she just left. I never knew who she was. But I keep going back to that. That one person standing still was enough. It changed what I think a show is for. It's not about everyone. It's about whoever stops.

2024 — Met someone through a screen

We met in a comment section, which is the least romantic sentence I can think of. Someone had posted a film still and I said something about the color of the light and they replied with this long thing about the same scene that I completely disagreed with. And we argued about it for, like, a week. Politely. Then it moved to messages. And then calls, where it was just voices at first, very late, in different time zones, so one of us was always tired. I knew what they sounded like when they laughed before I knew what their hands looked like. Um, it's strange to miss someone you've never been in a room with. But I did. I'd fall asleep with the call still going, just to hear that someone else was breathing somewhere. I don't really know what to call it, even now.

2025 — Finding the thread between poetry, film, and design

For a long time I felt like I had three separate lives. There was the poetry, which was private. The film stuff, which was mostly watching and taking notes. And design, which was the thing that was supposed to be a job. And I thought I had to pick one eventually, that I was kind of cheating on each of them with the others. And then I was working on something, I was editing a short sequence, and I realized I was cutting it the way I'd break a line in a poem. Holding on an image one beat too long, the way you leave white space after a stanza. And the layout I was doing that same week had the same rhythm. It wasn't three things. It was one way of paying attention, wearing different clothes. I almost laughed out loud. It felt like finding out two of your friends have known each other the whole time.

2025 — Found my muse

I don't love the word muse. It sounds like someone standing still while you look at them. And it's not that. It's someone who makes me want to notice things. Like we'll be walking and they'll stop because the light is doing something on a wall, and they won't say anything, they'll just stop. And I'll see it because they stopped. Most of what I've made this year has something of them in it, even when it's not about them at all. A color they wore. The way they hold a cup with both hands even when it isn't cold. Um. I worry sometimes that that's unfair to them, putting them into things without asking. So I've started asking. And they always say yes, and then they ask what I saw, and then I have to actually say it out loud. Which turns out to be the hardest part.

2026 — Still figuring out what love means

I thought by now I'd have, I don't know, a definition. Like it would be one of those things you just figure out by a certain age. And I've been in love, I think. I've definitely thought I was. But every time it looks different. Sometimes it's this huge thing that takes up the whole sky, and sometimes it's someone remembering how you take your tea. My grandparents were married for fifty years and I never once saw them hold hands, but she cut his hair in the kitchen every month until she couldn't anymore. Was that love? I think it was. I think it might be the most love I've ever seen. Um. So I don't know. I'm twenty-three. Maybe it's just one of those words you keep redefining for your whole life. Maybe that's the point of it, that it never sits still long enough to be finished.
